# Sources

Every entry in `seals.json` traces to a first-hand, public-domain source.
We record the source verbatim and never add interpretation.

## Seed sources (used for entries H-001 … H-005)

1. **Daya Ram Sahni, 1926.** *Annual Report of the Archaeological Survey of
   India 1923-24.* The first Harappa seals report. Public domain.
   - Transcribed via harappa.com's digitization of the ASI text, cross-checked
     against the report's plate/page numbers.

2. **Madho Sarup Vats, 1940.** *Excavations at Harappa: Being an account of
   archaeological excavations at Harappa carried out between the years 1920-21
   and 1933-34. Volume I – Text; Volume II – 139 Plates.* Public domain.

## Next sources to ingest (all public domain)

3. **John Marshall (ed.), 1931.** *Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization.*
   3 volumes. Public domain — full scans on archive.org.

4. **Ernest Mackay.** *Further Excavations at Mohenjo-daro* (1938). Public domain.

5. **M. S. Vats plate volume** — Vol. II's 139 plates contain the seal
   photographs for the Harappa entries; thumbnails can be cropped from the
   archive.org scan.

## Deliberately NOT copied

- **Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions (CISI)**, Vols. 1–3
  (Joshi & Parpola 1987; Shah & Parpola 1991; Parpola et al. 2010).
  The definitive photographic corpus — but copyrighted. We cite it as a
  reference; we do not reproduce its photos.

## How to add a source

1. Confirm it's public domain or openly licensed (pre-1930 publications
   generally are; always check).
2. Add the bibliographic entry above with a number.
3. Cite it in each new `seals.json` entry's `source` field.
